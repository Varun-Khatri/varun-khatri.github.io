Chapters 4 + 5 — outlined (flesh out after Ch3 playtest)

Ch4 — Collapse (3 levels)

Level 4-1: All six mechanics compound — no more sandboxes. Level 4-2: Time-reverse puzzle rooms (new mechanic, teach + apply same level). Level 4-3: X4 set-piece (time-reversed fold, reassemble the room). Module mix: G+F+H combinations. Module roots follow same Z-chaining convention.

Ch5 — Resolution (2 levels + ending)

Level 5-1: Reuses Ch1–3 module geometry dressed in hybrid world state (ghost-blended materials). Level 5-2: X5 final set-piece. No new mechanics — purely narrative payoff. Module positions mirror 1-1 to 1-3 spatially (player recognises the space, now broken open).

Visual style

Overall style

Stylised realism. Not photorealistic, not cartoon. Reference: Control (brutalist architecture + supernatural) meets Ori and the Will of the Wisps (warm organic light). Low polygon count with high-quality PBR materials. Readable silhouettes — the player must instantly understand what is geometry and what is decoration.

Rendering

Unity URP (Universal Render Pipeline). Enables custom shaders for the fold distortion, portal render textures, and world-blend materials. Target 72 fps on Quest 3 standalone. Mobile URP profile: baked lighting where possible, one real-time directional light per world state.

Hybrid world (Ch3+)

Ghost blend shader

A custom URP shader that lerps between the Overgrown and Sterile material sets via a single float parameter (0 = full Overgrown, 1 = full Sterile, 0.5 = 50/50 ghost blend). The blend is driven by the WorldStateManager. In hybrid zones, geometry from both worlds is visible simultaneously at 60% opacity each — slight additive bloom on the overlap seam. Lighting blends the two directional lights by the same float. This shader must be written first — all other world-state visuals derive from it.

Design decision: how does the player switch worlds?

Answer: Wrist-flip switch — not a Dishonored timepiece

The Dishonored 2 timepiece is a held object the player raises and swipes — it requires a persistent prop in-hand. Echo Fold uses the gloves themselves as the interface. No held item. The world-switch is a deliberate wrist gesture, readable but not accidental. Full detail below.

The world-switch gesture

Input: double wrist-flip

Player flips either wrist palm-down → palm-up (pronation to supination) twice within 0.6 seconds. The second flip confirms intent — prevents accidental switches during other gestures. Detection: wrist rotation delta > 140° on the roll axis within the time window. Works on either hand.

Why not a button or joystick?

Echo Fold uses hand-tracking (no controllers). There are no buttons. All input is gesture-based. The wrist-flip was chosen because: (1) it reads as a "reveal" or "turn over" motion — thematically appropriate, (2) it uses a different axis than all other gestures so there is no conflict, (3) it can be done while holding an Anchored object, (4) it feels satisfying in VR.

World switch — transition sequence (140 ms total)
0 ms
Gesture detected
GestureDetector fires WorldSwitchIntent event. WorldStateManager receives it.
0–30 ms
Haptic pulse
Both wrist haptics (if controller fallback) or audio-only (hand tracking). A sharp double-click sound plays — Sterile: electronic click, Overgrown: organic thud.
0–60 ms
Glove FX fires
The glove mesh emits a ripple of light outward from the palm — teal rings expanding. This is the visual "cast" of the switch.
60–120 ms
World dissolve
WorldObject components set new active state. Materials crossfade via ghost blend shader (blend float 0→1 or 1→0 over 60 ms). Lighting lerps directional light colour + fog colour simultaneously.
120–140 ms
Audio bed crossfade
Overgrown ambient (birds, wind, distant water) and Sterile ambient (hum, faint fan, subtle beep) crossfade over 20 ms trailing window. Player is now fully in the new world.
What persists across world switches

Persists (always present)

  • Player position and velocity — no teleport or reposition on switch
  • Anchored objects (held in place by gesture) — they stay frozen
  • Shared platform colliders (WorldObject=Both)
  • Portal frames already opened by Scale gesture
  • Receiver pads and their fill state
  • Audio log playback if mid-log

Does not persist (world-specific)

  • WorldObject=Overgrown / WorldObject=Sterile platforms
  • Hazard objects tagged to a single world
  • Lighting, fog, ambient audio
  • Materials on shared geometry (shader crossfades)
  • Pulled objects that haven't been Anchored yet — they return to their world if mid-pull when switch fires
Switch cooldown + edge cases

Switch cooldown: 1.2 seconds after a switch to prevent rapid flipping. If the player is mid-pull when a switch fires, the pull is cancelled and the object returns. If the player would be standing in empty air after a switch (the floor they're on is world-specific and doesn't exist in the new world), the switch is blocked and a subtle visual feedback (glove glow dims, denied sound) tells them why. This is the only case where a switch is blocked.

Pull mechanic — full spec

It is not like the Dishonored 2 timepiece

The Dishonored timepiece is a passive viewport — you hold it up and see through time. Echo Fold's Pull is an active extraction — you reach through a portal boundary and physically drag an object across the world boundary into your space. The spatial metaphor is closer to reaching through a window and pulling something off a shelf on the other side.

Pull gesture sequence
1
Locate a pullable object
Pullable objects (tagged Pullable, glowing amber #D4A017) are visible through portal windows or floating in the other world. A teal outline #1ABC9C appears on the object when the player's hand is within 2 m of a portal surface and pointing generally toward the object.
2
Reach gesture
Player extends their arm toward the portal window. When the hand crosses the portal plane (or enters a PullZone trigger collider if no explicit portal frame), the hand visually distorts — fingertips show a teal ripple indicating they have crossed the boundary. No need to physically reach inside a portal — the gesture is detected from 0.5 m away.
3
Grip close
Player closes their hand (all fingers curl, tracked via hand joint data — metacarpal to proximal phalanx angle > 60°). If a pullable object is within the reach cone (30° half-angle from finger direction, 3 m max), it is grabbed. The object spawns a "pull trail" — a teal ribbon from the object to the player's hand, showing it crossing the world boundary.
4
Pull motion
Player draws their hand back toward their body. The object follows the hand position 1:1 in the player's world space — it has emerged from the portal. Physics is disabled on the pulled object (kinematic) while held. The pull trail shrinks as the object crosses over.
5
Release / Anchor
Open hand → object drops with normal physics in the player's current world. Or: hold open palm (Anchor gesture) → object freezes in mid-air, physics disabled, teal aura maintained. World assimilation: after 3 seconds held or Anchored in the new world, the object's material crossfades from its source world material to the current world material — it "belongs" here now.
Pull constraints and rules

Distance + weight

Max pull range: 3 m from portal surface. Objects tagged Heavy (large puzzle slabs) require two-handed grip — both hands must close simultaneously. Heavy objects move at 60% of hand speed and have slight lag. Light objects (cubes, tablets) are 1:1.

Mid-pull world switch

If player switches world mid-pull, the pull is cancelled. The object returns to its origin position over 0.5 s with a "snap back" spring animation. Cooldown prevents immediately re-pulling. This is intentional — world-switch and Pull are separate gestures that don't overlap.

Pull without a portal frame

In some puzzle rooms (G-type), objects are visible through a semi-transparent world-boundary plane rather than an explicit portal frame. Pull still works — the boundary plane acts as the portal surface. The plane emits a soft teal shimmer when the player's hand approaches.

Object return

Pulled objects that are dropped (not Anchored) and fall off a platform after 3 s return to their origin position via a "recall" — they dissolve where they are and re-materialise at their start point. Prevents permanent softlocks. Anchored objects that are un-anchored (hand moved away) follow normal physics and do not auto-return.

Visual language of a pull

The pull trail is a key visual. It is a ribbon mesh generated procedurally between the object and the portal frame edge, made of the object's world colour (amber for Overgrown, blue for Sterile) fading to teal at the player's hand. The ribbon shimmers and has a slight chromatic aberration on its edges — the object is between worlds. When the object fully crosses over, the ribbon snaps with a particle burst (teal + world colour mix) and the object's material begins crossfading. This effect should be the most polished VFX in the game — it is the mechanic players will do hundreds of times.

Audio philosophy

No music during active gameplay. Ambient soundscapes only. Music (diegetic — comes from broken speakers, old radios) plays only in safe zones between modules. This keeps the player attentive to spatial audio cues that are part of puzzle feedback. A receiver pad accepting an object has a distinctive sound — this is as important as the visual feedback.

Ambient layers per world

Overgrown ambient stack

  • Layer 1 (always): distant wind through broken walls
  • Layer 2: dripping water, location-aware (louder near flooded modules)
  • Layer 3: birdsong (sparse, single calls, not loops)
  • Layer 4: vine/branch creak on player movement
  • Layer 5 (puzzle rooms only): low sub-bass hum — the world is under tension

Sterile ambient stack

  • Layer 1 (always): HVAC hum, 60 Hz electrical buzz
  • Layer 2: distant server fans, pitch varies by proximity to server rack props
  • Layer 3: occasional console beep (random, non-rhythmic)
  • Layer 4: footstep echo off hard floors (longer reverb tail)
  • Layer 5 (puzzle rooms only): same low sub-bass hum as Overgrown — same source, different timbre
Gesture sound design
Pull
Reach: soft tonal whoosh. Grab: tactile click + teal chime. Pull trail active: a continuous low shimmer tone, pitch rises as object gets closer to crossing over. Cross-over: sharp harmonic burst. Release: soft thud (object lands in new world).
Rotate
Ratchet-style click every 15° of rotation. Near-alignment: tone rises in pitch. Locked: satisfying clunk.
Scale
Expanding: glassy rising tone. Collapsing: same tone descending. Portal walkable threshold (when scale enables collision): distinct harmonic ping.
Fold
Paper-fold crumple layered with a deep resonant boom. The world shifting physically has weight and cost. Most dramatic sound in the game.
Anchor
Magnetic lock: metallic click + held sustain tone while object is frozen. Release: spring release snap.
World switch
Sterile→Overgrown: wet organic thud + exhale. Overgrown→Sterile: electronic crack + sharp inhale. Both are short (<200 ms) and punchy — not drawn out.
Player body and locomotion

Locomotion: smooth + teleport hybrid

Default: smooth locomotion via head-direction + hand lean (no thumbstick — hand tracking). Walk speed: 2.5 m/s. Run: lean forward >20° from vertical, speed 4 m/s. Teleport: aim beam + open palm hold 0.5 s on valid surface → blink teleport (15 ms fade to black + back). Both available — player chooses via comfort settings.

Jumping

Crouch (lower head 0.3 m below neutral) then raise quickly → jump impulse. Height: 1.2 m. No controller button needed. Alternatively: physical crouch-jump in real space (room-scale mode). Jump has forgiving coyote time: 0.15 s off-edge still allows jump. Landing: slight camera dip 0.05 m over 80 ms.

Comfort settings

Vignette on movement (adjustable 0–100%). Snap turn available (15°/22.5°/30° increments). Teleport-only mode for high motion-sickness sensitivity. All platform heights and gap distances are tuned for the default smooth locomotion — teleport-only players may find some jumps trivially easy (teleport ignores jump physics).

Player body

No visible body (avoids VR body tracking mismatch nausea). Gloves only visible — high-fidelity hand mesh with per-finger joint tracking visualisation. Gloves are the character. Their material shifts subtly with world state: warm amber tint in Overgrown, cool blue sheen in Sterile. In hybrid state: both tints pulse alternately.

Death + respawn
Falling
No fall damage. Player falls into a void below a module → 1 s slow-motion fall, then fade to black (0.5 s), respawn at module entry checkpoint. No penalty. Philosophy: death should never be frustrating in VR — disorientation from unexpected resets compounds nausea.
Hazard contact
Tagged Hazard objects trigger a screen-edge red flash + screen shake 0.02 m / 0.1 s. First contact: warning only, pushed back 0.5 m. Second contact within 3 s: respawn at checkpoint. Generous because VR makes spatial judgement harder than flat screens.
Checkpoints
Auto-checkpoint at: (1) start of each module, (2) after each puzzle solved (receiver filled), (3) 15 s after entering a new module if no puzzle present. Checkpoint saves: world state, all receiver fill states, collected audio logs. Pulled objects reset to their start position on respawn.
Softlock prevention

Any object tagged Pullable that has been dropped off the map or into an unreachable location auto-returns to spawn after 4 s (dissolve + rematerialise). Any receiver that is permanently blocked resets its required object to spawn after 10 s of inactivity. A "reset puzzle" option in the pause menu resets the current module's objects to spawn without respawning the player. This must be implemented before any playtesting.

Questions answered — locked decisions
Is the world-switch like the Dishonored 2 timepiece?
No. The timepiece is a held prop you slide. Echo Fold's switch is a body gesture (double wrist-flip) — no prop, no UI. The spatial metaphor is different: Corvo reveals the past by holding up a window; Asha switches between two co-existing states by flipping her own perception. Thematically important distinction.
Can the player see both worlds at the same time outside of set-pieces?
Only through portal frames (deliberate view windows). Outside portal frames, only one world is fully visible. Ghost geometry of the other world is faintly visible as 15% opacity outlines — enough to plan ahead, not enough to read clearly. Ch3+ hybrid zones break this rule intentionally.
What happens to a pulled object if the player switches world while holding it?
Pull is cancelled. Object returns to spawn. Gesture conflict resolution: world-switch always cancels any in-progress gesture except Anchor (Anchor persists across switch).
How does the player know what is interactable?
Three visual affordance layers: (1) Pullable objects glow amber #D4A017 at all times. (2) A teal outline appears when the hand is within interaction range. (3) A faint particle effect (floating specs) orbits pullable objects. Nothing else has all three of these. Rotate handles glow amber + pulse. Portal frames always emit teal. No HUD or tooltips — all spatial.
Is there a HUD?
No. Fully diegetic. World state is communicated by environment (lighting, fog, materials, ambient audio). Puzzle progress is communicated by receiver pad fill state (empty = dim, filled = bright teal glow). Checkpoint save is communicated by a subtle glove pulse. No HP bar, no objective tracker, no minimap.
How does the player know which world they are in?
Five simultaneous signals: (1) lighting colour temperature, (2) fog colour, (3) ambient audio, (4) glove tint, (5) material set of all surfaces. Any one of these should be enough. Having all five ensures the player never loses track, even in peripheral vision.
What is the target playtime?
Ch1–3: ~3 hours (5 fully built levels × ~35 min each). Ch4–5: ~2 hours. Total: ~5 hours for a focused playthrough. VR attention fatigue is real — each session should feel completable in one sitting (one level = 30–40 min). No level should exceed 45 min. The modular structure means levels can be tuned by adding/removing modules.
Is there a narrative reason for the Fold Gloves?
Yes — established in Ch1 audio logs. Asha developed them as part of the quantum resonance experiment. They were designed to interface with the dual-state field the experiment created. After the split, they are the only objects that exist in both worlds simultaneously (they were worn during the event). This is why Asha can interact with either world — and why the gloves are the visual representation of the player's agency.
What engine systems need to be built first, before any level work?
In order: (1) WorldStateManager + WorldObject component (everything depends on this), (2) GestureDetector (all input), (3) Ghost blend shader (all visuals), (4) Portal render texture setup, (5) Pull system (PhysicsObject + PullTrail VFX), (6) Anchor system, (7) Receiver + locked door system, (8) Checkpoint + respawn system, (9) Object auto-return system. Only after all nine are prototyped should module prefab building begin. Build all nine with grey primitives and placeholder materials first.
What free assets are needed immediately?
Priority: (1) Modular sci-fi interior kit for Sterile geometry (Kenney sci-fi pack or Quaternius), (2) Ruin/concrete rubble for Overgrown (Kenney or Sketchfab CC0), (3) PBR concrete, metal, moss textures (Polyhaven), (4) Hand mesh for gloves (Oculus Sample Framework hand mesh — free, usable), (5) Basic ambient sound loops (Freesound CC0: wind, HVAC hum, drip). None of these block grey-box development — all can be swapped in after core systems are working.
Controller fallback — what if hand tracking fails or player uses Quest controllers?
Full controller mapping: Right grip = Pull grab. Right thumbstick press = world switch. Both grips + twist = Rotate. Both grips + spread/pinch = Scale. Left grip + hold = Anchor. Right trigger + flick = Script-trigger. Fold (two-finger pinch) = Left + Right trigger simultaneously. Controller mode disables gesture detection and enables button mapping. This doubles development input testing surface — test both from day one.
